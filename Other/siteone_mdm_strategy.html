What Master Data Management actually means

Master Data Management is not a software product and it is not an IT project. It is the organizational decision to pick one trusted version of your most important business facts — who your customers are, who your suppliers are, what you own, what you sell — and treat that version as the authority across every system, team, and process.

Done right, MDM is invisible. A branch manager sees accurate hours on Google. A buyer knows exactly which supplier to call. A customer gets an offer for the product they actually buy, not one they've never touched. The friction that everyone currently works around disappears because the data underneath is clean.

Why SiteOne needs this now

SiteOne's core POS system was built in 2005. It was not designed for a company doing $16M/day across hundreds of locations, with 10–14 acquisitions per year each bringing their own customer lists, supplier relationships, location records, and product catalogs. The result is predictable: duplicate customer records living in both UE and Salesforce with no reconciliation logic, a supplier with up to eight records across US and Canadian systems, branch hours that are wrong in one system and right in another, and a product catalog where the base-variant model is inconsistently applied.

The pain is not abstract. Personalization at scale requires knowing which Account owns which contacts — that linkage is currently broken. Procurement contracts require knowing your true supplier count — that number is currently unknown. Delivery routing requires clean fleet and location data together — those live in four or five separate systems. Every acquisition accelerates all of these problems simultaneously.

The four domains, and how they connect

SiteOne's master data lives across four domains. Each domain is a business capability — and none of them fully works without the others.

Domain 01 — Customer

A customer is a node, not a name.

The Account — a contractor, landscaper, nursery, or commercial buyer — is SiteOne's core unit of relationship. Every contact, every transaction, every service history hangs off that Account. Today, that linkage is broken.

Where things stand today

Customer records live in both UE and Salesforce without a reconciliation layer — so the same contractor may exist as three records in one system and two in another, with no golden record to arbitrate. The definition of "customer" varies by department: for the branch, it's the account; for marketing, it's the contact; for finance, it's a billing entity. That definitional gap means no one system can drive personalization at scale.

Account-to-contact linkage is currently broken. A contractor's account exists, and their buyer's email exists, but the connection between them is unreliable. Without that link, personalized communication — the right offer to the right person at the right time — is not possible at SiteOne's scale. The team currently runs a manual data cleaning cycle once a year. That is not a process; it is a temporary measure against entropy.

The fundamental problem: SiteOne cannot scale personalization because it cannot reliably answer a simple question — which contacts belong to which account? Until the Account ↔ Contact linkage is resolved into a single trusted record, every personalization and retention initiative is building on sand.

Business value unlock

Cross-sell and up-sell
A trusted Account record with full purchase history is the prerequisite for product recommendations. Know what a contractor buys, and you can intelligently surface what they don't.
Personalization at scale
One customer, one message — across branch and digital channels. Resolving contacts to accounts is the unlock; once it's done, SiteOne can deliver communication that feels like a relationship, not a blast.
Small customer growth
Knowing which accounts you actually have — deduplicated and attributed — is the prerequisite for knowing which accounts you don't. Whitespace analysis requires clean data.
Customer retention
Understanding why accounts go quiet, what drives stickiness, and where churn risk concentrates requires a consistent account record over time. You can't analyze what you can't trust.
M&A onboarding
AI-assisted match and classify incoming customer records from acquired branches. With a clean golden record as the target, acquisition onboarding becomes a workflow rather than a project.

The entities being mastered

Customer Domain
Account
The master business customer record
The Account represents a contractor, landscaper, nursery, or commercial buyer — it is the foundational unit of SiteOne's customer universe. Every transaction, contact, and service history ties back to an Account. Attributes include account type (contractor, retail, wholesale), region, assigned branch, credit tier, annual spend, acquisition source, and active/inactive status. In the current state, duplicates across UE and Salesforce mean this foundational record is not reliable. MDM makes it the single source of truth.
Customer Domain
Contact
An individual associated with an Account
The Contact is the buyer, decision-maker, or job site contact — a real person associated with an Account. Attributes include name, email, phone, role and title, preferred communication channel, opt-in status, and last interaction date. Currently, contacts are duplicated across UE and Salesforce due to system constraints and inconsistent sync logic. The account-to-contact linkage is the single most important relationship to resolve in the Customer domain — it is what makes personalization, retention analysis, and lifecycle marketing possible.
Domain 02 — Supplier

Eight records.
One supplier.

SiteOne's procurement relationships are real and deep. The data reflecting those relationships is not. A single vendor can exist as up to eight separate records across US and Canadian systems, with no link between them.

Where things stand today

The supplier onboarding form has minimal validation — fields are free-text, formats vary, and there is no deduplication logic at point of entry. The result is a supplier base where the true count is unknown. Procurement teams work around this by maintaining their own spreadsheets, which diverge from the system of record over time. M&A compounds the problem: each acquisition brings its own supplier relationships, some of which overlap with SiteOne's existing vendors at different price points, terms, and contacts.

The question SiteOne cannot currently answer: How many distinct suppliers do we have? Until that question has a reliable answer, contract compliance, cost benchmarking, and private brand expansion are all operating on guesswork.

Business value unlock

Procurement process improvement
A single trusted supplier record enables contract compliance tracking and cost benchmarking across the full supplier base — not just the portion any one team can see.
M&A supplier deduplication
Understand supplier overlap before and after acquisition close. Identify where you're paying two prices for the same product from the same vendor — and consolidate.
Private brand expansion
SiteOne's private brand ambitions require clean supplier-to-product linkage. You cannot build a private label program on top of fragmented supplier data.
Supply risk management
Know which products source from which suppliers, and which locations handle which product types. Geographic supply risk becomes visible and manageable.

The entities being mastered

Supplier Domain
Supplier
The master vendor or manufacturer record
The Supplier is the authoritative record for a vendor or manufacturer SiteOne does business with. Attributes include legal name, tax ID, supplier type (national, regional, private label), primary contact, payment terms, preferred currency, active/inactive status, and compliance certifications. Currently fragmented into multiple records per vendor across systems — with no reconciliation layer. A single golden Supplier record is the foundation for everything procurement depends on.
Supplier Domain
Supplier Location
A specific operating site or warehouse
A Supplier Location is a specific warehouse, distribution center, or regional office belonging to a Supplier. Attributes include address, timezone, dock capabilities, hazmat handling certification, supported product lines, and lead time by location. This entity enables SiteOne to route purchase orders to the right supplier site, understand geographic supply risk, and know which locations can support which product requirements — including hazmat and specialty items.
Domain 03 — Asset

Start here.
The recommended first domain.

SiteOne's assets span two types: the physical locations where business happens, and the fleet and equipment tied to those locations. This domain was identified as the recommended MDM starting point — lower disruption, clearer ROI, and foundational for every other domain.

Where things stand today

Location data — branch hours, addresses, timezone, dock capability — lives in multiple disconnected systems. Timezone and hours errors are not hypothetical: they cause missed customer calls, delivery routing failures, and incorrect information surfaced to customers looking for branch details. Fleet and equipment data is tracked across four or five separate systems with no master record and no reliable way to know which asset is assigned to which location.

The org hierarchy — Division → Region → Area → Branch — must be consistent for financial rollups and operational reporting. In the current state, that hierarchy can differ across systems, making consolidated reporting an exercise in manual reconciliation before any analysis can begin.

Why start here: The Asset domain has fewer stakeholders than Customer or Product, and the value is immediate and measurable. Clean branch data improves customer-facing accuracy today. Clean fleet data improves delivery routing tomorrow. And because every acquisition brings 1–4 new locations, a repeatable onboarding process here pays dividends 10–14 times a year.

Business value unlock

Delivery operations
Route planning and order tracking require trusted fleet and location data together. Today those live in separate systems; unifying them is a direct delivery optimization win.
Last-mile fulfillment
A branch that knows its own dock capability, service area, and truck compatibility can make accurate delivery promises. Incorrect capability data leads to failed deliveries and customer service escalations.
M&A location onboarding
Each acquisition brings 1–4 new locations needing rapid, clean data setup. A standard onboarding template with validation rules means new locations are live and accurate in hours, not weeks.
Reporting and rollup
The org hierarchy (Division → Region → Area → Branch) must be consistent for financial and operational reporting. Clean location master data makes consolidated views automatic, not manual.

The entities being mastered

Asset Domain
Branch / Location
A SiteOne operating location
A Branch or Location is an operating site in SiteOne's network — a standard branch, nursery, stone center, or distribution center. Attributes include location type, org hierarchy (division/region/area/branch), address, timezone, hours of operation, dock capability, truck compatibility, hazmat certification, cost center, and acquisition source. Currently maintained in multiple disconnected systems with errors in timezone and hours that directly impact customer service. This is the foundational location record that every other domain references.
Asset Domain
Asset
A physical asset owned or operated by SiteOne
An Asset is a delivery vehicle, forklift, trailer, or equipment unit assigned to a SiteOne location. Attributes include asset type, assigned location, make/model/year, capacity, license plate and VIN, maintenance schedule, and operational status. Currently tracked across four to five disconnected systems with no single master record. This entity is the starting point for MDM because it has fewer stakeholders than Customer or Product, and because clean fleet data delivers measurable delivery optimization value quickly.
Domain 04 — Product

The most mature domain.
With real work still ahead.

SiteOne's product domain has the most infrastructure in place — Athena handles deduplication and AI-driven attribution. But the base-variant model is inconsistently applied, the packaging hierarchy is unclear, and private brand expansion requires cleaner supplier linkage than currently exists.

Where things stand today

The internal platform Athena performs product deduplication and AI-driven attribute attribution — a real capability advantage over the other domains. PIMcore is in use but has limitations that have led to workarounds in how the base-variant model is applied. The result: SKU records that are inconsistent in how they relate to their parent product, packaging hierarchies that differ by category, and a category taxonomy that doesn't yet cleanly support private brand separation.

The stakes here are material. Product data quality directly affects inventory accuracy, delivery planning (what can fit on what truck), cross-sell recommendations, and the private brand expansion program that requires clean product-to-supplier linkage as its foundation.

The Athena advantage: SiteOne already has AI-driven product attribution in production. The MDM work here is not building from scratch — it is tightening the model that Athena feeds, so the AI's output is consistently correct across every product type, including the private brand SKUs where the stakes are highest.

Business value unlock

Planogram and inventory accuracy
A clean base-variant model means the right product is represented in the right place, with the right count. Inconsistent base-variant relationships are a root cause of inventory discrepancies.
Private brand expansion
Private brand SKUs require clean product records linked to the right supplier. The Category entity is also needed to flag private label eligibility at the category level.
Procurement and pricing
A clean category hierarchy enables contract negotiation and benchmarking by category — you can see what you're buying, from whom, and at what price, grouped in a way that means something to buyers.
Cross-sell and up-sell
Recommendation engines depend on clean category and variant data. The more consistently products are classified, the more relevant the recommendations — and the higher the attachment rate.

The entities being mastered

Product Domain
Base Product
The core product identity
A Base Product is the anchor record — the core product identity independent of packaging or variant. Attributes include product name, brand (SiteOne private label or national brand), primary category, supplier link, regulatory and hazmat classification, and product lifecycle status. All variants and pack configurations hang off the Base Product. Inconsistencies in how Base Products are defined today propagate into every downstream system that touches the catalog.
Product Domain
Variant / SKU
A specific sellable version
A Variant or SKU is a specific sellable version of a Base Product defined by size, color, grade, or species. Attributes include SKU code, variant descriptor (e.g., "5-gallon", "red", "Grade A"), unit of measure, weight, dimensions, and active/discontinued status. The current base-variant model has inconsistencies — some variants are treated as independent products, others as subordinate records — that affect inventory accuracy and cross-sell logic.
Product Domain
Category
The product classification structure
A Category is the node in SiteOne's assortment hierarchy that organizes products for planning, pricing, and reporting. Attributes include category name, parent category, department, and whether it is eligible for private label. A clean hierarchy enables smarter buying decisions, cleaner reporting, and the ability to manage private brand eligibility at the right level of granularity.
Product Domain
Pack Configuration
The defined ordering and stocking unit
A Pack Configuration defines the unit by which a product is ordered, stocked, or sold — each, case, pallet, or bundle. Attributes include pack type, quantity per pack, pack weight, and ordering unit of measure. Critical for branch-level ordering accuracy and distribution planning; a mismatch between how something is ordered and how it's stocked is a frequent source of inventory error.
Cross-Domain View

The sum is greater
than the parts.

Each domain has value on its own. But the real capability of a unified MDM program is in the connections between domains — the places where clean data from one domain enables something in another that neither could do alone.

Cross-domain relationship map

The five connections that matter

Customer ↔ Product: purchase history and cross-sell

Knowing what a contractor buys — precisely, at the SKU level — drives personalized recommendations and private brand push. This connection is currently underexploited because the Account record is fragmented. Fix the Account, and the product data that already exists in the catalog can be put to work driving revenue.

Supplier ↔ Product: every product traces to a supplier

Clean product-to-supplier linkage is the foundation for procurement optimization — benchmarking, contract compliance, and category-level negotiation. It is also the prerequisite for private brand expansion: SiteOne cannot build a private label program without knowing which supplier produces which product.

Asset (Branch) ↔ Customer: the home branch relationship

A customer is served by their home branch. Branch capabilities determine what can be promised: delivery radius, hazmat handling, specialty product availability, dock timing. Without a trusted Branch record, SiteOne cannot reliably know what it can promise to which accounts — and every broken promise is a retention risk.

Asset (Fleet) ↔ Product: what's on the truck

Delivery routing is not just about distance — it's about load compatibility. Hazmat products require certified vehicles. Bulk landscape materials require specific truck types and capacities. Knowing what's on the truck requires clean fleet data; knowing what can go on the truck requires clean product data. These two domains must speak the same language for last-mile fulfillment to work.

M&A as the forcing function

Every acquisition SiteOne makes — 10 to 14 per year — touches all four domains simultaneously. Customer records must be matched and merged. Supplier relationships must be reconciled for overlap. New locations must be onboarded cleanly. Product catalogs must be integrated. Today, each of these happens as a bespoke project. A unified MDM program transforms acquisition onboarding from a four-workstream project into a repeatable, accelerating process. At SiteOne's acquisition pace, this is not a nice-to-have. It is an operational requirement.

Ten to fourteen times a year, every domain breaks at once.

An MDM program is not just about data quality in the steady state. At SiteOne's acquisition pace, each new deal is a stress test of every domain simultaneously. The companies that do M&A at scale without a data foundation spend months cleaning up after each deal. The companies with a foundation spend days. That gap compounds across 10 to 14 deals per year.
