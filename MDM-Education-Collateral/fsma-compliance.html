📦 The Common Language of Food Safety

GS1 Standards:
The Vocabulary FSMA Speaks

FSMA Rule 204 doesn't mandate GS1, but it functionally requires the same thing: a universal, unambiguous identifier for every product, every location, and every movement event. GS1 is how compliant trading partners speak the same language — and how MDM gives every identifier a single authoritative home.

GTIN
Global Trade Item Number

The universal product identifier — 14 digits that uniquely identify any food item regardless of which retailer, distributor, or system is looking at it. The Canonical Anchor in MDM is built on the GTIN as its primary key.

GTIN-14: 00012345678905
→ Organic Romaine 6oz bag, Brand X
GLN
Global Location Number

A 13-digit identifier for any physical location or legal entity — farm, distribution center, store, or digital endpoint. Critical for FSMA CTEs: you cannot record a "receiving" event without knowing exactly where receiving happened.

GLN: 0614141000005
→ DC 7, Fresno CA, Receiving Dock 3
SSCC
Serial Shipping Container Code

An 18-digit identifier for a logistics unit — a pallet, case, or shipping container. Links physical shipments to the GTIN and GLN records in your MDM, enabling lot-level traceability across the supply chain.

SSCC: 006141410000050000
→ Pallet 5000, Shipment 2024-10-14
GS1-128 / 2D
Data Carriers & 2D Barcodes

2D barcodes (QR, DataMatrix) encode GTIN, lot number, expiry date, and serial number in a single scan. The FDA's "New Era" blueprint calls for industry-wide adoption by 2027 — enabling real-time, item-level recall traceability at the shelf.

Encodes: GTIN + Lot + Expiry
→ Full KDE set in one scan
GLN · PARTY
Supplier & Party Identification

Under FSMA's Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP), you must verify every supplier meets U.S. safety standards. GLN-based party identification in MDM ties certifications, audit records, and compliance documentation directly to a single supplier record.

Supplier CA-00-4471 → GLN 0614141...
→ GAP cert · Organic · FSMA verified
RFID · EPC
Electronic Product Code

RFID tags carrying EPCs enable carton-level inventory visibility without line-of-sight scanning. Combined with EPCIS event sharing, EPCs let every trading partner see the same movement history — essential for multi-party traceability under Rule 204.

EPC → RFID tag on case
→ Auto-captured at every CTE
🔗
EPCIS 2.0
Electronic Product Code Information Services

The standard that enables trading partners to automatically capture and share supply chain event data across different systems. EPCIS events are the language of CTEs — a "shipping" EPCIS event at the grower becomes a "receiving" event at your DC, automatically reconciled against your MDM's GTIN and GLN records. Without accurate master data as the backbone, EPCIS events are uninterpretable.

🔄
MDM as the GS1 Backbone
Why Transactional Data Needs Master Data

Every EPCIS event, every ASN, every lot code record is meaningless without accurate master data to interpret it. Your MDM system is the canonical registry that maps GTIN → product specification, GLN → location record, and Supplier ID → FSVP compliance status. Without it, GS1 standards generate data that no system can consistently read.

Workflow & Change Accountability

FSMA · 21 CFR Part 117 · Audit Logs

Specification management enforces approval workflows for every recipe update, artwork revision, and formula change — with a strict audit log of who changed what, when, and why. No unapproved modifications reach production.

Compliance Outcome

"If a mislabeling recall occurs, the FDA will ask who approved the label change and when. MDM/PIM gives you a timestamped answer."

Every specification change — a new allergen substituted, a different packaging film approved, a formula reformulation — goes through a governed workflow before it becomes active. The audit log is immutable and timestamps every approval. In an FDA inspection, this is the difference between demonstrating a controlled process and being unable to explain how an unapproved change reached a shipped product.
